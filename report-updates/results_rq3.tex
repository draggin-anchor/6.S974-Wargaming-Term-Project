\begin{minipage}[t]{0.51\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}
\textbf{Weather:} bad weather helps Blue slightly. Most policies do worst in \emph{calm} seas, since rough seas halve
Red's coast-guard strength and storms pause operations and incidents (MIP-BR war rate $0.24$ calm, $0.10$ extreme).
The policy ranking is robust. MIP-BR ($\varepsilon=0.40$) and equilibrium are first and second in all four
scenarios, so the best policy on average is also best in the worst case. The top scorer also has the most
wars ($0.12$--$0.29$), so $W=200$ may be too small.
\end{minipage}

\textbf{Designs:} De-escalation measures (D3) are best in expected and worst-case terms and cut the war rate fourfold
($0.20\to0.05$, nominal). They also raise Red's payoff ($101\to144$), since fewer wars help both sides. We charged them
nothing, so part of this win is by assumption. Workshop 2 will give them a cost. Dispersed basing (D1) barely
changes Blue's payoff but cuts Red's ($101\to16$), at a higher war rate ($0.28$). Redundant sensing (D2) shows no
measurable benefit even at zero cost, because better Blue information barely changes Blue's decisions in this model.
\textbf{Limitations:} parameters are assumptions, and policies look one period ahead. The war rate depends strongly on
$h$, $\mu$ and $W$, and payoff differences below about 20 are ties (s.e.\ 5--12).
