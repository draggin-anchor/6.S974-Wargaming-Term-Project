\textbf{Weather.} Bad weather helps Blue slightly: most policies do worst in \emph{calm} seas (Fig.~\ref{fig:rq3}), since
rough seas halve the effect of Red's coast-guard hulls and storms pause operations and incidents (MIP-BR war rate $0.24$ calm, $0.10$ extreme). The ranking is robust: MIP-BR ($\varepsilon=0.40$) and
equilibrium are first and second in all four scenarios, and the only change below them is a swap within noise.
So the best policy on average is also best in the worst case. Caution: the top scorer also has the most wars
($0.12$--$0.29$), so $W=200$ may be too small.

\textbf{Designs.} The hotline (D3) is best in expected and worst-case terms and cuts the war rate fourfold
($0.20\to0.05$, nominal). It also raises Red's payoff ($101\to144$): fewer wars help both sides. We charged it
nothing, so part of this win is by assumption; Workshop 2 will give it a cost. Dispersed basing (D1) barely
changes Blue's payoff but cuts Red's ($101\to16$), at a higher war rate ($0.28$). Redundant sensing (D2) shows no
measurable benefit even at zero cost: better Blue information barely changes Blue's decisions in this model.
