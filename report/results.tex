Analytical results use the restricted template game. Simulations use the same 200 seed identifiers per
configuration. Unless specified, Red uses MIP-BR with $\varepsilon_\Rd=0.15$ in nominal weather.

\subsection{Supporting analysis: incentives and repeated-game restraint}
Fix Blue's center-heavy and Red's south-heavy splits. Normalizing $(P,P)$ to $(0,0)$ gives
$(P,A)=(-9.61,1.20)$, $(A,P)=(0.28,-10.45)$ and $(A,A)=(-11.41,-11.33)$ (Blue, Red).
This chicken-type game has two pure equilibria. In the mixed equilibrium, assertion probabilities are
$0.576$ (Blue), $0.135$ (Red), with payoffs $v_\B=-1.30$, $v_\Rd=-6.02$.
A welfare-maximizing correlated equilibrium avoids mutual assertion.

In an \emph{infinitely repeated, perfectly monitored version of this fixed subgame}, patrol followed by
permanent mixed-equilibrium punishment after any assertion is sustainable when
$\delta\ge G_i/(G_i-v_i)$, with deviation gains $G_\B=0.28$, $G_\Rd=1.20$.
Thresholds $0.18$ and $0.17$ support restraint at $\delta=0.98$ in this auxiliary game,
not necessarily in the finite, partially observed simulator.

\subsection{Template-game equilibria}
Strict dominance reduces $14\times14$ templates to $2\times4$. Pure equilibria are (Blue center-heavy
assert, Red south-heavy patrol), payoffs $(0.39,-9.27)$, and (Blue center-heavy patrol, Red center/south-heavy
assert), $(-14.44,2.71)$. The tested policy selects the first by total payoff. A separate zero-sum control
game (Red receives minus Blue control payoff) has both sides assert in the Center, value $-2.26$.
