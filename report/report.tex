\section{Setting and research questions}
%======================================================================
\textbf{Contested system.} China and the United States compete for day-to-day control of the waters inside the
First Island Chain (Senkakus, Taiwan Strait, Luzon Strait, Scarborough and Second Thomas Shoals, Paracels)
\emph{below the threshold of war}. China keeps persistent presence with coast guard and maritime militia
(CCG) hulls backed by navy (PLAN) ships; the U.S.\ keeps a smaller number of destroyers (DDG) on presence
and freedom-of-navigation patrols \citep{helmus2024,green2017}. Each side decides, every two days,
\emph{where} to station its ships on a network of zones and \emph{how} to behave there: patrol (P) or
assert (A: shadowing, blocking, water cannon, close maneuvering).

\textbf{Why strategic interaction is central.} Control of a zone depends on the local force ratio, so each
side's best allocation depends on the other's; escalation risk is joint (incidents need both sides present, and
war costs both); and neither side sees the other's whole fleet.

\textbf{Research questions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}]
\item \emph{Control vs.\ escalation.} Each side's allocation is a MIP with an escalation-risk budget
$\varepsilon_i$. How much control does a larger $\varepsilon_i$ buy, at what war risk, and which budgets are
equilibrium choices when both sides pick them?
\item \emph{Strategic vs.\ heuristic play.} Do game-theoretic policies (equilibrium and maximin strategies,
exact best responses) beat random and rule-based ones, and can mutual restraint be sustained in repeated play?
\item \emph{Robustness and redesign.} Is the best policy or Blue design under nominal conditions also the most
robust to weather (Nature's scenario $\omega$), in expected and worst-case terms?
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Outcomes} (averaged over 200 seeds): Blue's value-weighted control share
$\bar c_\B=\frac1\tau\sum_t\sum_{v}V^\B_v c^t_v/\sum_v V^\B_v\in[-1,1]$, war rate, payoffs $u_\B,u_\Rd$, and for RQ3
the expected and worst-case payoff over $\omega$.

\section{Literature and current practice}
%======================================================================
\textbf{Documented practice.} \citet{helmus2024} catalog China's gray-zone tactics: persistent CCG and militia
presence, shadowing, blocking resupply at Second Thomas Shoal, water cannon, and non-navy hulls that stay below
the threshold of armed response. \citet{green2017} argue that deterrence below war depends on presence and on
managing escalation. U.S.\ practice is routine presence and freedom-of-navigation patrols, with encounters managed
by the Code for Unplanned Encounters at Sea \citep{cues2014}. We take from these the action set (presence,
patrol vs.\ assert), the asymmetric hull types, and the idea that incidents, not shots, drive escalation.
\textbf{Models.} \citet{wasser2019} design a seminar gray-zone game with explicit objectives and action menus;
we make every adjudication rule computable. The U.S.\ Coast Guard schedules randomized patrols from a game model
\citep{shieh2012}; zero-sum network games model transit and blockade \citep{washburn1995}; escalation as a shared,
manipulated risk \citep{schelling1966} is what our incident hazard makes explicit.
\textbf{What we add:} a general-sum game where both sides contest control at once and war is a joint cost,
with explicit rules so strategies and designs can be compared over thousands of runs. Numerical values are our
assumptions (Table~\ref{tab:params}), not documented data.

\section{Game formulation}
%======================================================================
\subsection{Agents, network and state}
Players $\I=\{\B,\Rd\}$. Ship types $\K_\B=\{\text{DDG}\}$ ($10$ hulls), $\K_\Rd=\{\text{PLAN},\text{CCG}\}$
($8$ and $6$). The theater is an undirected graph $G=(\V,\E)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:net}) with Blue home ports
$H_\B=\{\text{YOK},\text{GUA}\}$, Red home ports $H_\Rd=\{\text{NBO},\text{SAN}\}$ and eight contested zones $\C$
grouped into regions North $=\{\text{SEN},\text{MIY}\}$, Center $=\{\text{TWN},\text{EOT},\text{LUZ}\}$,
South $=\{\text{SCB},\text{STS},\text{PAR}\}$. One period is two days; an edge takes one period.
The state at period $t$ is $s^t=(x^t,d^t,c^t,\ell^t,w^t)$, where $x^t_{k,v}\in\Z_{\ge0}$ counts type-$k$ ships at node $v$; $d^t$ gives each ship's remaining endurance
(periods at sea, at most $D_k$); $c^t_v\in[-1,1]$ is the control index of zone $v$ ($+1$ Blue, $-1$ Red);
$\ell^t\in\{0,\dots,L\}$ is the escalation level ($L=5$ is war); and $w^t_r\in\{\text{calm},\text{rough},\text{storm}\}$
is the sea state of region $r$.

\subsection{Actions, constraints, information and uncertainty}
\textbf{Actions.} Each period player $i$ chooses for every free ship a destination at most one edge away
(staying is allowed) and a posture $p_{i,v}\in\{\Pt,\As\}$ at every zone it occupies. Writing $f^t_{k,uv}$ for the
number of type-$k$ ships moving from $u$ to $v$, the feasible set is defined by
\begin{align*}
&x^{t+1}_{k,u}=x^t_{k,u}+\textstyle\sum_{v\in\delta(u)}f^t_{k,vu}-\sum_{v\in\delta(u)}f^t_{k,uv}
&&\text{(flow conservation, Lecture 2)}\\
&\textstyle\sum_{k,(u,v)}f^t_{k,uv}\le F_i &&\text{(fuel: } F_\B=8,\ F_\Rd=10 \text{ moves/period)}\\
&d\le\operatorname{dist}(v,H_i)\Rightarrow\text{ship heads home};\ d\leftarrow D_k\text{ after a period in port} &&\text{(endurance)}
\end{align*}
and no ship may enter the opponent's home port or a storm-closed zone.

\textbf{Information.} Each player observes its own ships, $c^t$, $\ell^t$ and $w^t$ (public). It detects the
opponent's ships at $v$ with probability $q_{i,v}=0.95$ if it has a ship at $v$, $0.6$ if adjacent, $0.2$ otherwise
(satellite); Red has $q\ge0.6$ at four zones near its coast; rough or storm seas multiply $q$ by $0.7$.
A player's \emph{belief} is the last detected count at each node, so players move simultaneously at
information sets that do not reveal the opponent's positions (Lectures 5--8).

\textbf{Uncertainty (Nature).} Weather: in scenario $\omega\in\Omega=\{\text{calm},\text{nominal},\text{typhoon},\text{extreme}\}$
(weights $\pi_\omega=(0.25,0.40,0.25,0.10)$, Lecture 1), each region starts a 3-period storm w.p.\
$p_s(\omega)\in\{0,0.02,0.06,0.12\}$ and is otherwise rough w.p.\ $p_r(\omega)\in\{0,0.2,0.35,0.5\}$. A storm closes
the region's zones and freezes their control; rough seas halve CCG effectiveness and degrade detection. Incidents are also random (below).

\subsection{Transition dynamics, payoffs and termination}
\textbf{Control.} Local strength is $S_{i,v}=\sum_{k\in\K_i}w_k\,\alpha^{\1[p_{i,v}=\As]}x_{k,v}$ with
$w_{\text{DDG}}=w_{\text{PLAN}}=1$, $w_{\text{CCG}}=0.6$ ($0.3$ in rough seas), $\alpha=1.5$. With
$\phi_v=(S_{\B,v}-S_{\Rd,v})/(S_{\B,v}+S_{\Rd,v}+\kappa)$,
\begin{equation}
c^{t+1}_v=(1-\lambda)c^t_v+\lambda\phi_v,\qquad\lambda=0.3,\ \kappa=1,\label{eq:control}
\end{equation}
so control is earned by persistent presence and decays when a side leaves.

\textbf{Incidents and escalation.} Where both sides are present, an incident occurs with probability
$\rho_v=1-e^{-h_v}$, where
\begin{equation}
h_v=\big(h_0+h^\B_\As\1[p_{\B,v}=\As]+h^\Rd_\As\1[p_{\Rd,v}=\As]\big)\min\{S_{\B,v},S_{\Rd,v}\}\,(1+\ell^t/2).\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}
Each incident raises $\ell$ by one; a period without incidents lowers $\ell$ by one with probability $\mu=0.25$.
The factor $(1+\ell/2)$ makes crises self-reinforcing.

\textbf{Payoffs.} With $\sigma_\B=+1$, $\sigma_\Rd=-1$ and $m^t_i$ the number of moves,
\begin{equation}
g_i^t=\sigma_i\textstyle\sum_{v\in\C}V^i_v\,c^{t+1}_v-\gamma\,m^t_i-\eta\,\ell^{t+1},\qquad
u_i=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\delta^{t-1}g_i^t-W\,\1[\ell^\tau=L],\label{eq:payoff}
\end{equation}
where $\tau=\min\{T,\ \text{first } t \text{ with } \ell^t=L\}$ (termination by war or horizon). The war penalty $W$
hits both players, which makes the game general-sum. Zone values $V^i_v$ differ by player (Table~\ref{tab:params}).

\subsection{Decision models: the best-response MIP and the template game}\label{sec:mip}
\textbf{Best response as a MIP (Lecture 2).} Given its beliefs $\hat x_{-i}$ and the sea state, player $i$
chooses binary moves $y_{s,w}$ (ship $s$ goes to node $w$) and, at every zone, one binary option
$b_{v,(n,p)}$ meaning ``I will have $n$ hulls at $v$ with posture $p$''. Because $\phi_v$ and $\rho_v$ are nonlinear
in the counts, we tabulate them for every option, which makes the problem linear:
\begin{align*}
\max_{y,b,x}\ \ &\textstyle\sum_{v\in\C}\sum_{(n,p)}a_v(n,p)\,b_{v,(n,p)}-\gamma\sum_{s,\,w\ne\text{loc}(s)}y_{s,w}\\
\text{s.t.}\ \ &\textstyle\sum_w y_{s,w}=1\ \ \forall s;\quad \sum_{s,\,w\ne\text{loc}(s)}y_{s,w}\le F_i;\quad
x_{k,v}=\bar x_{k,v}+\sum_{s\text{ of type }k}y_{s,v};\\
&\textstyle\sum_{(n,p)}b_{v,(n,p)}=1,\quad \sum_{(n,p)}n_k\,b_{v,(n,p)}=x_{k,v}\ \ \forall v,k;\qquad
\sum_{v}\sum_{(n,p)}\rho_v(n,p)\,b_{v,(n,p)}\le\varepsilon_i\,(1-\ell^t/L);\\
&y,b\in\{0,1\},
\end{align*}
where $a_v(n,p)=V^i_v\lambda H\,\sigma_i\phi_v(n,p)-\Lambda\rho_v(n,p)$, $H=1/(1-\delta(1-\lambda))$ converts a one-period
change in $\phi$ into its discounted effect on control through \eqref{eq:control}, $\Lambda=20$ is the cost a player
assigns to one expected incident, and $\bar x$ counts ships on forced returns. The last constraint is the
\textbf{escalation-risk budget}: its feasible region is exactly the set of allocations whose expected number of
incidents stays below $\varepsilon_i$, tightened as $\ell$ rises. The proposal's ``conservative, moderate,
aggressive'' types become $\varepsilon_i\in\{0.05,0.15,0.40\}$. We solve it with Gurobi, as in the lecture code.

\textbf{Template game (Lectures 3--4).} To compute equilibria we abstract each player's strategy to one of 14
templates: a split of deployable ships across North/Center/South (seven splits) times a posture (P or A).
This gives a finite strategic-form game $\Gamma=\langle\I,(S_i),(u_i)\rangle$ with $|S_i|=14$ whose payoffs
$M_i(a_\B,a_\Rd)=\sigma_i\sum_vV^i_v\phi_v-\Lambda\sum_v\rho_v$ are evaluated with \eqref{eq:control}--\eqref{eq:hazard}
at steady state (calm seas, $\ell=1$). We analyze it with iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies (IESDS),
pure-NE enumeration, the maximin LP, and the correlated-equilibrium LP.

\textbf{Policies.} \emph{random}; \emph{moderate} (rule-based: asserts only with local superiority);
\emph{greedy} (fast MIP heuristic); \emph{MIP-BR}$(\varepsilon)$; \emph{equilibrium} (its part of a pure NE of
$\Gamma$); \emph{maximin} (samples its maximin mixed strategy of $\Gamma$).

\subsection{Resilient redesigns (Blue)}\label{sec:designs}
Each changes the system, not the operating policy: \textbf{D1 dispersed basing} adds a Blue port at Subic
(EDCA access) with edges to SCB, LUZ, STS (alternative paths and shorter cycles; cost 0.3/period);
\textbf{D2 redundant sensing} gives Blue a detection floor of $0.6$ in every zone, also in bad weather
(cost 0.5/period); \textbf{D3 crisis hotline} (CUES-style communication) raises the de-escalation probability to
$\mu=0.5$ and halves assert hazards once $\ell\ge3$ (no cost to Blue, but it also helps Red).

\textbf{Simplifying assumptions.} Two unitary players; control is a force-ratio index; incidents are the only
path to war; beliefs are last-seen counts; best responses look one period ahead.
